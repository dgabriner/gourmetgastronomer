# Benchmarks

Patterns to learn from — not visual clones, not competitors to beat.

## Encyclopedias (Wikipedia, specialist wikis)

**Learn:** stable IDs, hatnotes for disambiguation, “see also” that is actually related, citations next to claims.  
**Do not imitate:** infobox sprawl, drive-by tone, unlimited hatnotes.  
**Here:** `[[gg:]]` plus a capped related list and a short inbound list.

## Culinary teaching (King Arthur Baking School notes, Calvel)

**Learn:** baker’s percent as a native language; DDT as a control loop; “measure, then adjust.”  
**Do not imitate:** brand voice, kit upsells, or treating one mill’s flour as universal.  
**Here:** formulas derive hydration when grams exist; DDT is a canonical professional page.

## Food safety (NCHFP, extension)

**Learn:** tested processes, altitude tables, explicit “do not invent a jam.”  
**Do not imitate:** turning every page into a warning label.  
**Here:** `safety` asides and `recipe.safety`; never improvise times.

## Field guides / atlases

**Learn:** thin place records, geography as context for a living thing, historical entries kept after closure.  
**Do not imitate:** stars, hours, “best of.”  
**Here:** `operating` on places; Cowgirl’s closed shop stays knowledge.

## Long-form editorial (serious magazines, not recipe SEO)

**Learn:** a lede that defines, sections that earn their heading, pull-quotes used almost never.  
**Do not imitate:** 2,000 words of preamble.  
**Here:** nine obligations in `EDITORIAL.md`; delete swap-the-name paragraphs.
