Baking is a heat craft and a time craft. In bread, the baker manages flour, water, salt, and a leavening culture so that [[gg:topic:fermentation]] and oven spring produce a loaf worth eating. Professionals also manage [[gg:topic:desired-dough-temperature]] so that mix, bulk, and the clock stay in the same conversation.

This branch of Gourmet Gastronomer starts with [[gg:topic:sourdough]], because sourdough concentrates the questions that run through the rest of baking: what flour is doing, how dough gains strength, and how heat finishes the work. [[gg:topic:milling]] is the prior question — how the kernel became that flour. Calvel’s account of breadmaking is a technical companion for those questions, not a recipe book to copy [src:gg:source:calvel-taste-of-bread].

A bakery in the atlas, such as [[gg:place:tartine-bakery]] or [[gg:place:acme-bread]], is a place where those questions are answered in public, several times a day. The civic myth of the city’s loaf is [[gg:topic:san-francisco-sourdough]].
