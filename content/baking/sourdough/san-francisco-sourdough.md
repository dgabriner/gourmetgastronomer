San Francisco sourdough is real twice: as a city’s long commercial habit of sour French bread, and as a 1971 USDA isolation of the microbes in that process. It is not real as a law of nature that only this fog can sour dough, or that a bacterium named for the city lives nowhere else.

## What bakeries actually did

French-style bakeries were part of Gold Rush San Francisco. Boudin’s own timeline dates the bakery to 1849, a move to Dupont (now Grant) in 1852, and an 1868 decision to keep leavening with mother dough after commercial cake yeast was widely available [src:gg:source:boudin-history]. The company’s Gold Rush origin for that mother dough is presented as belief (“It’s believed that Isidore Boudin received the mother dough from one of these 49ers”) [src:gg:source:boudin-history]. That is house history, not a lab notebook.

San Francisco’s Legacy Business staff report treats Boudin as founded in 1849 with a first storefront in 1852, and records that Louise Boudin saved the mother dough in the 1906 earthquake and fire [src:gg:source:sf-legacy-boudin]. Civic documentation of a business is not microbiology. It is evidence that a sour, naturally leavened loaf was a commercial product here for a long time — while much of American baking went over to manufactured yeast.

The popular loaf — large, blistered, very sour, often a round “French” bread — is a style shaped by bakeries, flour, and a market that wanted that tang. [[gg:place:tartine-bakery]] is a later artisan chapter of the same city, not a continuation of Wharf tourism bread. Both belong in the atlas; they are not the same object.

## What the laboratory actually isolated

In 1971, at USDA’s Western Regional Research Laboratory in Albany, California, Sugihara, Kline, and Miller sampled sour-dough French bread fermentations from five San Francisco bakeries. The leavening yeasts were not bakers’ yeast. Most isolates were maltose-negative, identified then as asporogenous *Saccharomyces exiguus* (*Torulopsis holmii*) [src:gg:source:sugihara-kline-1971-yeasts]. Names have moved since; the finding that stands is the ecology: a yeast that does not compete for maltose in an acidic dough.

Kline and Sugihara then isolated the souring bacteria: gram-positive rods that wanted maltose, produced lactic and acetic acids (acetic from 3 to 26% of total acid in their measurements) plus carbon dioxide, and were fussy about medium [src:gg:source:kline-sugihara-1971]. They hesitated even to file them neatly among heterofermentative lactobacilli. Later taxonomy named the species *Lactobacillus sanfrancisco* / *L. sanfranciscensis*. In 2020 the genus split moved it to *Fructilactobacillus sanfranciscensis* [src:gg:source:zheng-2020-lactobacillus]. The city is in the epithet because of those isolates, not because the organism is a municipal employee.

## Is it unique to San Francisco?

No. The species is a sourdough specialist found in traditional Type I doughs far from California. Comasio, Verce, Van Kerrebroeck, and De Vuyst, writing about bakeries in more than one country, treat the San Francisco pairing of *F. sanfranciscensis* with *Kazachstania humilis* as a famous consortium — and as one of several maltose-sharing LAB–yeast pairings [src:gg:source:comasio-sourdough-2020]. In their own samples, *F. sanfranciscensis* was the prevalent LAB in sourdoughs from two of three bakeries *in different countries* [src:gg:source:comasio-sourdough-2020]. House microbiota, flour, and backslopping select the community. Fog is not a required reagent.

The tourist sentence “the bacteria only live here” collapses three true statements into a false one: (1) the species was described from here; (2) SF commercial doughs of 1971 had a characteristic yeast–LAB pairing; (3) that pairing is an ecological type, not a geographic fence.

## What to do with this as a baker

If you want sourness, manage [[gg:topic:fermentation]]: temperature, hydration, flour, and time select lactic versus acetic notes. Calvel’s warning still applies on the [[gg:topic:lactic-acid-bacteria]] page — city’s name is not an acid profile. Maintain a [[gg:topic:sourdough-starter]].
