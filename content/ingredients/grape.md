A grape is not automatically wine. Table grapes, juice grapes, and wine grapes are breeding and farming choices: skin thickness, seed, sugar, acid, and whether you want to chew them. The same botanical family can become [[gg:topic:jelly]] (juice plus sugar plus pectin) or [[gg:topic:wine]] (yeast on must). Those are different technologies.

U.S. jelly identity lists grape among the fruit juices that can make a standardized jelly [src:gg:source:cfr-21-150-140-jelly]. Wine’s [[gg:topic:tannin]] is largely a skin-and-seed extraction story [src:gg:source:smith-tannin-ajgw-2015]. Eating a Thompson Seedless will not teach you Cabernet skins.

## Sugar and acid in the berry

Ripeness for wine is a sugar–acid–flavor compromise. More hang time usually means more sugar (more potential alcohol) and falling acid. Cool nights can hold acid; that is why coastal fog stories on [[gg:place:sonoma-county]] and [[gg:place:napa-valley]] matter to growers. An AVA on a label is a geography rule [src:gg:source:ttb-appellations], not a guarantee of how any one cluster tasted.

## What to notice

**Look.** Bloom on the skin, even color for the variety, no collapse.

**Feel.** Wine grapes often have thicker skins and noticeable seeds; that is tannin waiting.

**Taste.** Pulp sweetness versus skin bitterness. The seeds’ crunch is a ripeness clue for winemakers; it is not a home-jam instruction.

[[gg:ingredient:sugar]] in the grape is the fermentable. In jelly, sugar is also added as a gel partner. Do not confuse those roles. [[gg:topic:fermentation]] is the shared microbial idea; wine applies it to this fruit.
