Wheat flour is what a miller chose to keep from a wheat kernel and grind to a baking powder. In the usual white flour, that choice is mostly endosperm. Whole wheat flour keeps the kernel’s parts in the food. Neither bag is “wheat” the plant — that is [[gg:ingredient:wheat]] — and neither bag is [[gg:topic:gluten]] yet. Gluten is a network the baker develops with water and work.

In U.S. food law, cereal flours are standardized foods. Wheat flours and related products are grouped in 21 CFR Part 137 [src:gg:source:ecfr-21-cfr-137]. The legal names on a bag (flour, bread flour, whole wheat flour) are identity standards, not mill-brand poetry.

## Why bakers care

Flour is the structure, the food for fermentation, and most of the flavor of a lean loaf. Protein content is a useful clue and a poor religion. Calvel’s account of breadmaking flour is about behavior in dough — gluten, ash, enzymes — not a single number to worship [src:gg:source:calvel-taste-of-bread].

Ash, measured as inorganic residue after combustion, is an indirect indicator of residual bran because those minerals are more concentrated in bran than in endosperm [src:gg:source:usda-ars-swql-ash]. A baker who only reads protein is missing the mill’s cleanliness story. How the kernel was opened, sieved, and blended is [[gg:topic:milling]].

## What to notice in dough

Protein on the spec sheet predicts *tendency*, not a loaf.

<aside class="cue">
<p><strong>Feel.</strong> A bread flour typically takes more water before it turns slack, and it fights the hand longer in a mix. A lower-protein pastry or cake flour wets quickly and stays short. Whole wheat drinks water and shows bran specks; it also ferments as if it had more to eat, because it does.</p>
<p><strong>Look.</strong> White flour doughs gloss as gluten organizes. High-extraction and whole-wheat doughs stay duller and specked. That is bran, not a mixing failure.</p>
</aside>

Absorption — how much [[gg:ingredient:water]] a flour will hold while remaining shapable — moves with protein, damaged starch, and bran. The same “75% hydration” country formula is a different dough in August with a new lot. Adjust water to the flour in front of you; do not treat a percentage as a moral rule.

[[gg:ingredient:salt]] tightens gluten and slows fermentation. It is not a flour substitute.

## Professional reading of a spec

<aside class="pro-note">
<p>A bakery specification is a receiving document: protein, ash, moisture, falling number or another enzyme clue, and sometimes a mixograph or alveograph. Lot-to-lot drift is normal. Change water, mix time, or fermentation before you rewrite the formula. [[gg:place:giustos]] is a South San Francisco processor in that chain; [[gg:place:acme-bread]] publishes an organic-flour sourcing story — treat those pounds and varieties as producer claims.</p>
</aside>
