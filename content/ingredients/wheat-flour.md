In U.S. food law, cereal flours are standardized foods, not casual synonyms for “whatever white powder is in the bag.” Wheat flours and related products are grouped in 21 CFR Part 137 [src:gg:source:ecfr-21-cfr-137].

Protein is a useful baking clue and a poor religion. Calvel’s account of breadmaking flour is about behavior in dough — gluten, ash, enzymes — not a single number on a bag [src:gg:source:calvel-taste-of-bread].

See [[gg:ingredient:wheat]] for the plant, [[gg:topic:milling]] for how the kernel becomes streams, [[gg:topic:gluten]] for the network flour can form, and [[gg:topic:sourdough]] for one craft that depends on both. [[gg:place:giustos]] is a South San Francisco processor in that chain; [[gg:place:acme-bread]] publishes an organic-flour sourcing story — treat those pounds and varieties as producer claims.
