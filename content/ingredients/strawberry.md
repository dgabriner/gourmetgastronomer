Strawberry is a fruit you smell before you trust the color. For preserving, two facts pull in opposite directions: fully ripe berries taste like strawberries; fully ripe berries are grouped with fruits that need added acid, pectin, or both if you want a conventional gel [src:gg:source:ohioline-hyg-5350]. That is why the tested NCHFP jam uses crushed ripe fruit *and* a package of powdered pectin [src:gg:source:nchfp-strawberry-jam-pectin].

## What to notice

**Look.** Bright color, no wet collapse. White shoulders can mean underripe or a variety quirk.

**Smell.** The volatile is the quality test. Pale perfume means pale jam.

**Feel.** Soft enough to crush; not slime.

**Taste.** Sugar and acid in the berry become the jam’s baseline. A dull berry will not be rescued by boiling.

OSU’s old kitchen split — some people can fruit ¼ underripe for pectin — is a pectin strategy, not a flavor strategy [src:gg:source:ohioline-hyg-5350]. Commercial pectin lets you use ripe fruit and still set. Low-sugar pectins are a different contract; see [[gg:topic:pectin]].

California seasons put strawberries on Bay Area tables early and often; this page is not a harvest calendar. A market such as the [[gg:place:ferry-building]] is where city eaters meet fruit. Cook them as [[gg:topic:jam]]; can them only on a tested process ([[gg:recipe:strawberry-jam]]).
