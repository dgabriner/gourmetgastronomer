Milk is not generic white liquid. Species (cow, goat, sheep), breed, feed, season, and fat content change what [[gg:topic:cheese-cultures]] and a kettle can do with it. This page is the shared substance, not a dairy-science textbook.

FAO’s cheese notes split milk protein: casein about 75–80% of total protein, whey proteins about 20–25%. Cheesemaking coagulates casein; most whey protein leaves with the whey [src:gg:source:fao-small-scale-cheese]. Fat is an emulsion that gets trapped in the curd. Lactose is the sugar [[gg:topic:fermentation]] consumes in the vat. Heat history (pasteurization, cold storage) changes how easily the milk will set — a handling fact on [[gg:topic:cheese-coagulation]].

U.S. cheddar identity allows milk, nonfat milk, or cream as dairy ingredients, and if they are not pasteurized the cheese must be cured at not less than 35 °F for at least 60 days [src:gg:source:cfr-21-133-113-cheddar]. That is a standardized-cheese rule, not a home-cheesemaking license.

Bay Area milk is geography. [[gg:place:point-reyes-farmstead]] began as a Tomales Bay dairy shipping milk, then made cheese from the herd [src:gg:source:point-reyes-cheese-about]. [[gg:place:cowgirl-creamery]] presents North Bay cheesemaking as the public face of similar agriculture [src:gg:source:cowgirl-creamery-site]. The craft is [[gg:topic:cheese]].
