Sugar is not only sweet. In this encyclopedia it has at least three jobs, and mixing them up is how people invent unsafe jam.

**Gel and preservation in jam.** Ohio State: sugar must be in proportion with pectin and acid to make a gel; it also limits microorganisms and contributes taste [src:gg:source:ohioline-hyg-5350]. You cannot swap sucralose into a regular-pectin canned jam and keep the same process; NCHFP is explicit that Splenda does not provide sugar’s preservative properties, and regular pectin formulas require their sugar [src:gg:source:nchfp-ensuring-safe-canned-foods]. The NCHFP strawberry jam uses 8 cups sugar with 5½ cups crushed fruit [src:gg:source:nchfp-strawberry-jam-pectin]. Change that and you have left the tested process. See [[gg:topic:jam]] and [[gg:topic:pectin]].

**Fermentable in wine.** [[gg:ingredient:grape]] berries already hold glucose and fructose. Yeast turns much of that sugar into alcohol. Residual sugar is sweetness in the glass. Added sucrose in a kettle and native sugar in a grape are the same class of molecules in different foods. [[gg:topic:wine]] is the craft.

**Baking.** A little sugar in dough is food for yeast and color in the crust. It is not jam’s 1:1 fruit contract.

If you want shelf-stable canned jam, sugar is a formula ingredient, not a preference. [[gg:topic:food-safety]] before you reduce it.
