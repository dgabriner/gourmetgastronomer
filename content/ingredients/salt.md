Salt is a small percentage and a large effect. It is one substance with several jobs. This page is the shared mineral. Crafts apply it.

**Bread.** Salt seasons crumb, strengthens [[gg:topic:gluten]], and reins in yeast. Leave it out and dough behaves like a different food. When you add it — with the flour, after an autolyse, or dissolved in [[gg:ingredient:water]] — is a mixing choice [src:gg:source:calvel-taste-of-bread]. The teaching [[gg:recipe:country-loaf]] uses 2% of flour weight, a common baker’s-percentage starting point, not a moral law.

**Cheese.** FAO treats salting as its own stage: dry salt or brine [src:gg:source:fao-small-scale-cheese]. Salt flavors, continues whey expulsion, and changes which microbes thrive. CDR lists salt with moisture, fat, and pH as a composition you verify if you will age the cheese [src:gg:source:cdr-pipeline-2015-cheese]. Details: [[gg:topic:cheese-salting]].

**Preserving.** Many pickles are salt-forward; traditional jam is sugar-and-acid-forward. Do not assume a salty food is a canned food. See [[gg:topic:preserving]].

Taste salt as itself. Then notice what it did to the structure — tighter dough, tighter curd, firmer pickle — rather than only whether the dish is “seasoned.”
