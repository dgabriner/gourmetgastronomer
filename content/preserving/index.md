Preserving is a pact with time. Some methods only slow spoilage (refrigeration, drying, a high-sugar pot of jam in the fridge). [[gg:topic:canning]] is the narrower claim that a sealed jar is shelf-stable because a tested heat process was followed. Those are not the same job.

NCHFP, adapting USDA’s *Complete Guide to Home Canning*, puts the safety line at acidity. Acid foods (pH 4.6 or lower) — fruits, pickles, sauerkraut, jams, jellies, marmalades, fruit butters — can be processed in a boiling-water canner. Low-acid foods (pH above 4.6) — meats, most vegetables, milk — need pressure canning because *Clostridium botulinum* spores survive boiling-water temperatures [src:gg:source:nchfp-ensuring-safe-canned-foods]. [[gg:topic:acidity]] is the shared chemistry; this page is the preserving consequence.

## Sweet spreads are one branch

Ohio State Extension treats jams, jellies, preserves, conserves, marmalades, and fruit butters as gelled or thickened fruit products whose differences are fruit preparation and firmness, not different magics [src:gg:source:ohioline-hyg-5350]. [[gg:topic:jam]] is the flagship for how a pectin gel sets. [[gg:topic:jelly]] and [[gg:topic:marmalade]] are neighbors. [[gg:ingredient:sugar]] is structure and, in traditional high-sugar gels, part of preservation — not a flavor garnish.

## Cooked jam versus canned jam

You can cook fruit, sugar, and [[gg:topic:pectin]] into a gel and keep it cold. That is making jam. Putting that gel through a boiling-water process with a tested formula is making a canned product. The [[gg:recipe:strawberry-jam]] page is the second thing: NCHFP’s powdered-pectin strawberry jam, including altitude-adjusted process times [src:gg:source:nchfp-strawberry-jam-pectin]. Changing fruit, sugar, or acid leaves that process.

Paraffin is not a modern seal. Extension is explicit: boiling-water processing is what prevents mold at room temperature [src:gg:source:ohioline-hyg-5350].

Read [[gg:topic:food-safety]] before you light a canner. This branch will not invent process times.
