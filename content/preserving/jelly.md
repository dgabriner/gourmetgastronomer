Jelly is juice that has been convinced to stand up. Ohio State: jelly is usually cooked fruit juice with sugar; a good one is clear, holds its shape when unmolded, and still quivers [src:gg:source:ohioline-hyg-5350]. The U.S. standard of identity agrees on the material: jelly is a jelled food from filtered or strained fruit juice, finished at not less than 65% soluble solids, with at least 45 parts fruit-juice ingredient to 55 parts saccharine ingredient [src:gg:source:cfr-21-150-140-jelly]. Kitchen speech and the legal name overlap more here than they do for “preserves.”

## Why clarity is a process

You extract juice — often with heat and a jelly bag — then gel it. Pressing the bag gives more juice and cloudier jelly. Extension: the clearest jelly drips without squeezing [src:gg:source:ohioline-hyg-5350]. Overcooking fruit to extract juice can damage pectin, flavor, and color.

[[gg:ingredient:grape]] jelly is a classic because grapes can be both a juice crop and a wine crop. Those are different products from the same botanical family; wine is [[gg:topic:fermentation]] of must, not a pectin gel.

## Gel is still pectin, sugar, acid, concentration

The mechanism is the same conversation as [[gg:topic:jam]], with less insoluble fruit in the way. Doneness tests (sheet, plate, thermometer) are on the jam page; do not run a second copy here. Added [[gg:topic:pectin]] is common when juice is low in pectin.

## Canning

A firm jelly on a spoon is not automatically a shelf-stable canned food. Process only from a tested formula. See [[gg:topic:canning]] and [[gg:topic:preserving]].
