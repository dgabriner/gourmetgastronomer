Canning is a thermal process with a lid, not a vibe of “I boiled it a long time.” NCHFP’s safety page, adapted from USDA AIB 539, exists because *Clostridium botulinum* can grow in moist, low-acid, low-oxygen food between about 40 °F and 120 °F and produce toxin in days [src:gg:source:nchfp-ensuring-safe-canned-foods]. Spores are ordinary on raw food. Washing does not solve them. The researched process time does.

## Acid versus low-acid

pH 4.6 is the split [src:gg:source:nchfp-ensuring-safe-canned-foods]:

- **Acid foods** (pH 4.6 or below): most fruits, pickles, sauerkraut, jams, jellies, marmalades, fruit butters. Boiling-water canner, using the tested time for that food and jar size, with altitude adjustment.
- **Low-acid foods** (pH above 4.6): meats, seafood, poultry, milk, most vegetables. Pressure canner, 240–250 °F. Boiling-water “for hours” is not a substitute; USDA’s comparison is that a true boiling-water process for low-acid food would run on the order of 7–11 hours, which is not how home canners are instructed to work [src:gg:source:nchfp-ensuring-safe-canned-foods].

Tomatoes and figs can sit near the line; if canned as acid foods they must be acidified [src:gg:source:nchfp-ensuring-safe-canned-foods]. Do not guess a lemon-juice amount. Use the tested recipe.

[[gg:topic:acidity]] explains pH. This page is why preserving cares.

## Jam in a pot is not jam in a pantry

Cooking [[gg:topic:jam]] to a gel answers pectin, sugar, acid, and concentration. Sealing that gel for the shelf answers heat, headspace, and a tested process. The NCHFP strawberry jam with powdered pectin processes half-pints or pints 5 minutes at 0–1,000 ft, 10 minutes at 1,001–6,000 ft, 15 minutes above 6,000 ft [src:gg:source:nchfp-strawberry-jam-pectin]. Those minutes are not interchangeable with a long-boil no-pectin jam, a reduced-sugar spread, or an invented fruit mix.

Ohio State: all unrefrigerated fruit spreads must be boiling-water processed; paraffin is no longer recommended [src:gg:source:ohioline-hyg-5350].

## What this page will not do

It will not list process times for vegetables, meats, or untested jam. If two authorities disagree, use the tested process for your country and altitude; do not average them. See [[gg:topic:food-safety]] and [[gg:recipe:strawberry-jam]].
