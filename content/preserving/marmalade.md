Marmalade is a jelly that kept the peel. Ohio State: soft fruit jellies containing small pieces of fruit or peel; they often contain citrus [src:gg:source:ohioline-hyg-5350]. Culinary English sometimes treats “marmalade” as citrus-only; extension language is slightly broader. Either way, the peel is not a garnish. Citrus albedo is a pectin source, and the oil in the zest is aroma you cannot get from juice alone.

## Why citrus cooperates

Citrus is high in both [[gg:topic:pectin]] and [[gg:topic:acidity]] relative to a ripe strawberry. That is why marmalade can set without the same added-pectin dependence as strawberry [[gg:topic:jam]] — and why bitterness is a variable, not an accident. Pith and boiling time change how much bitterness you keep.

## Observation

Look for peel distributed through a clear gel, not a jammy sludge with a few strips on top. Taste bitterness separately from sweetness so you can decide whether the next batch needs thinner peel or a longer first boil of the rinds.

## Safety

Marmalades are listed among acid foods that *can* be boiling-water canned when you follow a tested process [src:gg:source:ohioline-hyg-5350]. This page does not supply one. See [[gg:topic:canning]].
