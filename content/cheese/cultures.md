A cheese culture is a chosen population, not a sourdough [[gg:topic:sourdough-starter]]. Both are [[gg:topic:fermentation]] tools. The habitat is different: milk instead of flour paste, usually a defined strain or blend instead of a continuously backslopped house flora.

Starter lactic acid bacteria convert lactose to lactic acid. That acid helps destablize casein, contributes to whey expulsion, and makes the vat a worse place for many competitors [src:gg:source:fao-small-scale-cheese]. U.S. cheddar identity still names this step in plain language: dairy ingredients are “subjected to the action of a lactic acid-producing bacterial culture” before clotting enzymes are added [src:gg:source:cfr-21-133-113-cheddar].

## Starter versus adjunct versus rind flora

- **Starter.** Acidification on a schedule. Mesophilic blends for many washed-curd and cheddar-family makes; thermophilic blends where the cook is hotter. The point is a predictable pH curve, not a tasting note.
- **Adjuncts.** Extra strains for flavor or openness (think propionic eyes in a Swiss-type make). They are not a second coagulation method.
- **Surface flora.** Geotrichum, Penicillium, Brevibacterium and their neighbors live on [[gg:topic:cheese-rind|rinds]]. They are cultures in the biological sense; they are not the vat starter.

Molds that marble a blue cheese are inoculated or encouraged later, often by piercing so air reaches the interior. That is a make choice, not “wild cave air” as an explanation.

## What to observe

Milk that was sweet becomes quietly tart. A clean yogurt-like smell is typical of a healthy acidification. A sluggish vat — pH barely moving, curd that will not firm — is a culture, temperature, antibiotic, or phage problem, not a reason to wait overnight and hope.

Feel the set after the culture has worked: acid-only gels are brittle compared with rennet gels [src:gg:source:fao-small-scale-cheese]. That difference is [[gg:topic:cheese-coagulation]], not a culture brand.

## Variables

Temperature, inoculation rate, milk heat-treatment, and residual antibiotics all move the clock. Faster acid can make a drier, more brittle curd; slow acid leaves a sweeter, wetter paste that [[gg:topic:affinage]] will not rescue if the style needed the opposite.
