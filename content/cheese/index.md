Cheese is [[gg:ingredient:milk]] made keepable. The liquid becomes a gel, the gel is broken so [[gg:topic:curd|curd]] can leave whey behind, and what remains is salted and either eaten young or given to [[gg:topic:affinage]]. That sequence is old craft and ordinary biochemistry, not a mystery of caves.

FAO training notes put the work in four stages: coagulation of casein, drainage of whey, salting, and ripening [src:gg:source:fao-small-scale-cheese]. U.S. identity standards for a named cheese such as cheddar still describe the same skeleton — lactic culture, a clotting enzyme, cutting and heating to separate whey, then salting and pressing [src:gg:source:cfr-21-133-113-cheddar]. Style is a set of choices inside that skeleton, not a different physics.

## What is being transformed

Milk’s casein can be destabilized by acid, by enzyme, or by both. Fat is trapped in the protein network. Lactose is food for [[gg:topic:cheese-cultures]]. Whey proteins mostly leave with the whey. What you keep — moisture, fat, salt, pH — is what later microbes and enzymes have to work with. For the mechanism of the set, see [[gg:topic:cheese-coagulation]]. For the biology of souring, see [[gg:topic:fermentation]] and [[gg:topic:lactic-acid-bacteria]]; this page does not reprint them.

## Why curd size and heat matter

Smaller pieces expose more surface, so whey leaves faster and the paste dries. Stirring and cooking continue that expulsion. That is why a moist bloomy cheese and a hard aged wheel are not the same vat taken to different calendars. They were cut and cooked differently. Observation belongs on [[gg:topic:curd]].

## Variables

Cheese texture and flavor depend on:

- the milk (species, fat, protein, hygiene)
- how far cultures take the acidity before and after the set
- acid versus rennet coagulation, or both
- curd size, cook, drain, and press
- how and when [[gg:ingredient:salt]] enters
- rind and aging room

Change one lever and the others move. A “60-day cheddar” is a legal aging floor for some unpasteurized standardized cheeses in the U.S., not a flavor guarantee [src:gg:source:cfr-21-133-113-cheddar].

## What to notice

Look at whey: cloudy early, clearer as protein is captured. Feel the curd: a clean break under a knife is a different material from a yogurt-like acid set. Smell the vat: clean lactic sour is information; barnyard or bitter solvent is a different population. Taste young curd for sweetness fading as lactose is consumed.

## Failure modes

Weak set, shattered curd, trapped whey, and late gas are quality problems with causes in milk, culture, temperature, or cutting. Pathogen control is not a sensory vibe. Pasteurization rules and aging rules are jurisdiction-specific; do not invent a home process that pretends to replace them.
