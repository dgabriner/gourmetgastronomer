Cheese tasting is [[gg:topic:tasting]] aimed at a dairy paste. The shared skill is slow attention. This page adds what cheese uniquely presents: a rind/paste contrast, a fat-and-salt matrix, and flavors that are often microbial rather than fruity.

CDR treats grading as a job: flavor, body, and texture checked when the cheese is young, then again during aging [src:gg:source:cdr-pipeline-2015-cheese]. You do not need a contest sheet to steal that habit. You need a knife and honesty.

## A usable sequence

1. **Look.** Paste color, eyes or slits, creamline, rind type. A gray paste under a bloomy coat is information; so is weeping oil.
2. **Smell the cut.** Not the refrigerator. Lactic, buttery, mushroom, washed-rind savory, ammonia, barnyard. Name only what is there.
3. **Feel.** Temperature first — cold fat hides aroma. Then texture: spring, paste, crunch of crystals, dryness of a natural rind.
4. **Taste.** Salt, acidity, bitterness, sweetness leftover from lactose, lingering animal or mushroom notes. Chew enough for fat to coat; then wait for the finish.
5. **Rind separately.** Some rinds are the point. Some are a dried skin you may not want a full bite of. Decide after you taste, not before.

## Variables that change the same cheese

Serving temperature, how long the piece has been cut, and what you ate before (especially [[gg:topic:wine-tasting|wine]] tannin and acid) all move perception. A wine that dries the mouth will make a young salty cheese taste saltier. That is physiology, not a pairing rule you must obey.
