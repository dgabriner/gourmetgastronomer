Wine tasting is [[gg:topic:tasting]] with a narrower object. The shared skill is naming only what you perceived. This page is a sequence for a glass, not a score, not a ranking of regions, and not a vocabulary performance.

## Look

Hold the glass against something white. Color depth in a red is not quality; it is often extraction and age. A young purple rim and a brick rim are different times in the same bottle’s life. Clarity matters for faults (haze, unexpected fizz in a still wine). Viscosity (“legs”) mostly tracks alcohol and sugar; it is a weak quality sermon.

## Smell

Short sniffs, not a vacuum. Fruit, floral, herbal, spice, oak, oxidation (sherry-like), reduction (struck match, which may blow off). Temperature hides aroma when the wine is too cold. A dirty glass is not the vineyard.

## Taste — map the physics

Sip enough to coat. Then separate the sensations:

- **Sweetness** at the tip — residual sugar, not “fruity aroma.” A dry wine can smell like berries and still finish without sugar.
- **[[gg:topic:acidity]]** as tartness and whether the mouth waters. Low acid tastes flabby; high acid tastes sharp. Same grape, different region and pick date, different acid.
- **[[gg:topic:tannin]]** as dryness and grip, especially on gums and inner cheeks. It is tactile. Smith, McRae, and Bindon: astringency is the drying, puckering feel from tannin–saliva protein binding; higher acidity makes the same tannin feel more astringent [src:gg:source:smith-tannin-ajgw-2015]. That is why a high-acid, tannic young red can feel harsher than a rounder one with similar phenolic numbers.
- **Alcohol** as warmth in the throat and a sense of body. Heat that shouts usually means the wine is warm or the alcohol is high relative to the rest of the structure.
- **Body** as weight — a combination of alcohol, extract, and sugar, not a synonym for quality.
- **Finish** as what remains after you swallow or spit. Length is interesting; so is whether bitterness or oak varnish takes over.

## Variables

Glass shape, serving temperature, whether the bottle was open yesterday, what you ate, and what you just smelled. Cheese fat and salt change the next sip; that is why [[gg:topic:cheese-tasting]] and this page should know each other, not copy each other.

## What not to do

Do not invent tasting notes for a wine you have not had. Do not treat “let it breathe 30 minutes” as a law; some wines open, some fall apart, some were fine in the first minute. Watch the glass.
