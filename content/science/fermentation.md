Fermentation is a living process, not a style. Microbes transform a food’s sugars into gas, [[gg:topic:acidity|acid]], alcohol, and flavor compounds. [[gg:topic:wild-yeast]] and [[gg:topic:lactic-acid-bacteria]] are two of the workers. Bread, cheese, and wine hire them for different jobs. They are not the same job.

**Bread.** In dough, fermentation is timed against gluten strength and oven schedule [src:gg:source:calvel-taste-of-bread]. Flour paste is the habitat. Application: [[gg:topic:bulk-fermentation]] and [[gg:topic:sourdough]].

**Cheese.** [[gg:ingredient:milk]] is the habitat; lactose is the sugar; cultures acidify and help the curd. Application: [[gg:topic:cheese]] and [[gg:topic:cheese-cultures]]. Do not copy the dairy make onto this page.

**Wine.** [[gg:ingredient:grape]] must is the habitat; glucose and fructose are the sugars; yeast makes ethanol. Application: [[gg:topic:wine]]. Spontaneous wine yeast and a sourdough starter are cousins, not twins.

Jam is usually not a ferment. Heat, sugar, and pectin are a different preservation chemistry — [[gg:topic:preserving]]. Pickles and vinegar would be the sour-preserving neighbors; they are not built here yet.

What to observe in any ferment: bubbles or gas, a sour or alcoholic smell, a change in taste, and whether the smell is clean. Time is a proxy. Temperature, inoculation, and the food’s composition move the clock.
