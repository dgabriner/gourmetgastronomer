Lactic acid bacteria (LAB) sour dough and milk. They produce lactic acid and, in heterofermentative species, often acetic acid and carbon dioxide as well. That [[gg:topic:acidity]] flavors food and makes the habitat harder for some competitors.

In a [[gg:topic:sourdough-starter]] LAB share flour paste with [[gg:topic:wild-yeast]]. In cheese they are often added as [[gg:topic:cheese-cultures]]. Wine’s main alcoholic ferment is yeast; malolactic conversion is a later bacterial story, not this page.

The lactic/acetic balance in bread depends on temperature, hydration, and flour — not on a city’s name alone [src:gg:source:calvel-taste-of-bread]. *Fructilactobacillus sanfranciscensis* (formerly *Lactobacillus sanfranciscensis*) was isolated from San Francisco bakery doughs in 1971 as a maltose-loving heterofermenter [src:gg:source:kline-sugihara-1971] and renamed in the 2020 *Lactobacillus* split [src:gg:source:zheng-2020-lactobacillus]. It is a sourdough specialist, not a municipal monopoly. History and the geographic myth: [[gg:topic:san-francisco-sourdough]].
