“Wild” yeast means not dosed from a foil packet. In a [[gg:topic:sourdough-starter]], yeasts live alongside [[gg:topic:lactic-acid-bacteria]] in a flour-and-water habitat. They produce much of the gas that raises bread. Flour — [[gg:ingredient:wheat-flour]] — is a major source of the microbes a starter captures [src:gg:source:calvel-taste-of-bread].

They are not a San Francisco monopoly. 1971 bakery sampling found maltose-negative yeasts (then *Saccharomyces exiguus* / *Torulopsis holmii*), not bakers’ yeast, doing the leavening in those sour French doughs [src:gg:source:sugihara-kline-1971-yeasts]. That ecology is famous, not exclusive. See [[gg:topic:san-francisco-sourdough]].

Wine yeast on grape must is a different habitat: more sugar, more alcohol, usually *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* when inoculated. Do not copy sourdough microbiology onto [[gg:topic:wine]]. Both are [[gg:topic:fermentation]].
