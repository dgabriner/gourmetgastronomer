Boudin is how many visitors first meet San Francisco bread: a sour round, a long company timeline, and a mother dough the house says it has kept. The bakery’s history page dates establishment to 1849, a North Beach address at 319 Dupont (Grant Avenue) in 1852, and an 1868 refusal to switch the bread to Fleischmann’s cake yeast [src:gg:source:boudin-history]. The same page states it is “believed” that Isidore Boudin received mother dough from a Forty-Niner [src:gg:source:boudin-history]. Belief is not a chain of custody.

The city’s Legacy Business staff report repeats the 1849 founding, notes that the first storefront was 1852, and records Louise saving the mother dough in 1906; it places later ownership with Stefano Giraudo from 1941 and calls the 10th Avenue and Geary site the oldest remaining San Francisco location [src:gg:source:sf-legacy-boudin]. The company now operates many California rooms, including tourist-facing bakeries. This atlas node is the institution, not a store locator. Hours and menus omitted.

Use Boudin to teach continuity of commercial sour bread in this city. Do not use it as proof that *Fructilactobacillus sanfranciscensis* lives only at Fisherman’s Wharf. That argument is on [[gg:topic:san-francisco-sourdough]]. [[gg:place:tartine-bakery]] is another SF bakery with a different public role. The craft is [[gg:topic:sourdough]].
