Napa Valley is a delimited grape-growing area, not a synonym for “expensive Cabernet.” Napa Valley Vintners state that the valley was named California’s first AVA in 1981, with nested AVAs inside it that name smaller geographies [src:gg:source:napavintners-ava]. TTB’s appellation system is the legal layer: an AVA is a viticultural area you may put on a wine label when the grape origin rules are met [src:gg:source:ttb-appellations]. Nested names are still geography. They are not recipes.

The association’s fact sheet describes a dry Mediterranean climate (a climate type they note is uncommon globally), fog that keeps summer nights cool, warmer northern valley days than southern ones, and a small share of California’s wine-grape harvest [src:gg:source:napa-valley-fast-facts]. Carneros, at the south, is the association’s example of marine air off San Pablo Bay; up-valley is less marine. That gradient is why [[gg:ingredient:grape]] varieties are not planted as wallpaper. Cooler sites hold [[gg:topic:acidity]]; warmer sites ripen different sugar and flavor curves.

This encyclopedia will not document every nested AVA. The teaching point is: twenty miles of valley is enough climate diversity to change wine, and San Francisco sits on the bay that helps cool the southern end. Taste with [[gg:topic:wine-tasting]].
